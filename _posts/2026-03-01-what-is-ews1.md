## 
What Does EWS1 Stand For?

EWS1 stands for External Wall System 1.
The form is completed by a qualified fire engineer or building surveyor after inspecting a residential building's external wall system, including its cladding, insulation, and balconies. The purpose of the EWS1 form is to give mortgage lenders confidence that they understand the fire safety risk of the external walls when deciding whether to lend on a property.

## 
Why Was EWS1 Introduced?


The EWS1 form was introduced as a direct response to the 
In the aftermath, mortgage lenders became reluctant to lend on flats in buildings with cladding systems unless they could satisfy themselves that the external wall system was safe or that any remediation costs were not going to fall to leaseholders and affect the value of the property.
Before EWS1 existed, there was no consistent, industry-wide process for assessing and documenting external wall systems. Lenders were applying their own inconsistent criteria, which meant many homeowners were simply unable to sell or remortgage. The EWS1 form was designed to resolve this by creating a single, standardised document that all major UK lenders could rely on.

## 
Is EWS1 a Legal or Government Requirement?


This is one of the most common misconceptions about EWS1. 
EWS1 is an industry-led process, driven by mortgage lenders and their valuers. A lender may require an EWS1 form before approving a mortgage on a flat, but no law compels building owners to obtain one. However, in practical terms, if your lender requires an EWS1 form and you cannot provide one, your property may be unmortgageable which in effect makes it unsellable.

## 
Which Buildings Need an EWS1 Assessment?


Not all buildings require an EWS1 form. RICS publishes guidance on when an EWS1 assessment should be requested by lenders. As of the most recent guidance (updated January 2022 and carried forward into 2025), an EWS1 form is typically required in the following situations:
**Buildings of any height where:**

Aluminium composite material (ACM) panels are present
Metal composite material (MCM) panels are present
High-pressure laminate (HPL) panels are present

**Buildings over six storeys where:**

There is cladding on the building
There is curtain wall glazing
There are combustible balconies

**Buildings of five or six storeys where:**

There is a significant amount of cladding (approximately one quarter or more of the elevation)
There are vertically stacked balconies containing combustible materials

## 
What Are the EWS1 Ratings?


The EWS1 form produces one of four ratings, split across two categories: 
Category A Ratings
**A1:** No cladding or cladding confirmed as non-combustible
**A2:** Cladding present but fire risk is sufficiently low
Category B Ratings
**B1:** Combustible cladding present but risk assessed as low
**B2:** Combustible cladding present and remediation required

## 
Frequently Asked Questions About EWS1


**Does every flat need an EWS1?**
No. Only flats in buildings that meet specific criteria relating to height, cladding type, and balcony construction will typically require an EWS1 form. Flats in buildings without cladding and without combustible balconies generally do not need one.

**Can I sell my flat without an EWS1?**
It depends on whether your buyer's lender requires one. If they do and you cannot provide a valid EWS1 form, the sale is likely to fall through. Cash buyers are not bound by lender requirements, so a cash sale may proceed without one.

**How do I know if my building already has an EWS1 form?**
Ask your managing agent or freeholder. RICS also recommends that all completed EWS1 forms are uploaded to the RICS portal, where they can be accessed by registered valuers.

**Is an EWS1 form the same as a fire risk assessment?**
No. A fire risk assessment (FRA) covers the common areas of a building and is a legal requirement. An EWS1 form specifically assesses the external wall system for mortgage valuation purposes. A building needs both.

**What is a FRAEW?**
A Fire Risk Appraisal of the External Wall (FRAEW) is a more detailed assessment carried out under PAS 9980 that informs the EWS1 process. Since 2022, RICS expects all EWS1 forms to have been completed after a FRAEW assessment.
